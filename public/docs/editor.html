Features

Drawing circuits

The schematic editor: parts, wires, net labels, editing values, and keeping your work.

The screen

  • Top bar: the Examples menu, New, Open, Save, Undo, Redo and Fit, plus the display toggles (current arrows, wire current, transistor regions). On the right is the simulation status: the number of steps, the step size and how long the run took to compute.
  • Toolbar (left): the select, wire, ruler and probe tools, then one button per part.
  • Canvas: the schematic and its rulers. A hint at the top says what the active tool expects next.
  • Inspector (right): whatever is selected. With nothing selected, it shows the simulation settings and a short how-to.
  • Time panel (bottom): the plot, the scrubber and playback. See Time and the plot.

Parts

Pick a part in the toolbar and click the canvas to place it. The tool stays active for placing more. Press R to rotate and F to flip the part before you click. Esc or a right-click goes back to the select tool. Each part gets the next free name (R1, R2, …) and a default value.

Wires and connections

Press W. Click to start a wire and click once at each corner. Clicking a pin or another wire finishes it. To stop anywhere else, double-click, press Esc or right-click.

Connections come from position, as in LTspice:

  • A pin and a wire end on the same grid point are connected.
  • A wire end or a pin lying on the middle of another wire makes a T-junction, drawn with a dot.
  • Two wires that only cross are not connected.

Net labels and node names

A net label names the node it touches. Labels with the same name are one node, so three Vcc labels connect without any wire between them. A label named GND joins ground. Nodes without a label are named N1, N2, …, and the ground node is GND. These names appear on rulers, in the plot legend and in tooltips.

Without a ground symbol the inspector warns you: voltages are then measured against an arbitrary reference.

Selecting, moving, rotating

  • Click to select a part, wire or ruler. ⇧-click adds to the selection, dragging on empty canvas draws a selection box, and ⌘A selects everything.
  • Drag a selection to move it. Wires attached to a moved part stretch to follow it. If a pin was touching another pin directly, a new wire is added so the connection survives.
  • R and F rotate and flip the selected parts. Wires don't move when you rotate. Since connectivity is by position, check the connections after rotating a part that's already wired.
  • ⌫ or Delete removes the selection, and so does the Delete button in the inspector.

Editing values

Select a part to edit its name and values in the inspector. A value commits when you press Enter or click elsewhere, and Esc abandons the edit. An invalid value turns the field red and isn't applied. Values use engineering notation, with an optional unit after the prefix: 4.7k, 10uF, 1meg, -2.5, 1e-6.

Moving around

  • Scroll to pan. Middle-drag also pans.
  • Pinch, or ⌘/Ctrl + scroll, to zoom.
  • Fit in the top bar frames the whole circuit and its rulers.

Saving and opening

  • The circuit autosaves to this browser's local storage, so a reload brings it back.
  • Save downloads circuit.json, with the parts, wires, rulers, probes and simulation settings. Open loads such a file.
  • New clears the canvas but keeps the simulation settings.
  • The Examples menu loads a ready-made circuit with rulers placed. It replaces the current circuit, which ⌘Z brings back. See Examples.
  • Undo and Redo cover every edit. A drag counts as one step.
