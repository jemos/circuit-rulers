Simulator and models

A small SPICE-like engine runs in your browser. It's made for small teaching circuits, not for sign-off accuracy.

How a run works

  1. Netlist. Pins, wire ends, T-junctions and same-named labels are merged into nodes, with ground as the 0 V reference.
  2. Starting point. Either the DC operating point, with capacitors open and inductors shorted, or the parts' initial conditions. If Newton–Raphson can't find the operating point directly, the solver falls back to source stepping, ramping every source up from zero in stages.
  3. Transient. The run then walks a fixed time grid using modified nodal analysis. Capacitors and inductors are integrated with the trapezoidal rule, or backward Euler if you choose it, and the first step always uses backward Euler. Each step is solved with Newton–Raphson, with SPICE-style pn-junction voltage limiting so the exponentials don't blow up. A step that won't converge is split in half, up to eight times, before the run gives up.
  4. Recording. Every node voltage and every pin current is stored at each grid point. That's what the rulers, plot, arrows and readouts read from.

If a run fails part-way, the results up to that point are kept and the top bar shows a ⚠ with the reason. A loop of voltage sources or inductors, or a source with both ends on the same node, makes the circuit unsolvable, and the message says so.

What it leaves out

  • No junction or gate capacitances, series resistances, Early effect on the BJT, temperature, or noise.
  • The op-amp has no bandwidth limit or slew rate: it's as fast as the time step.
  • Only transient analysis. There's no AC sweep or DC sweep, though a slow ramp or sine on a source does much the same job.
  • A fixed time step, at most 20 000 points per run. Pick the step for the fastest thing in the circuit.
  • Dense matrices, which is fine for tens of nodes and slow for hundreds.

Sign conventions

  • Two-pin parts have a + pin and a − pin. “V across” is V+ − V−, and “I (+ → −)” is the current entering the + pin.
  • Pin currents in readouts are the current flowing into the part at that pin.
  • A current source pushes its current out of its + pin, into the circuit.
  • Ruler steps are signed in path order: each node's voltage minus the next one's.
