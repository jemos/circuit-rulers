Examples

Load these from the Examples menu in the top bar. Each one comes with rulers already placed, usually a pinned one beside a live one. Loading an example replaces the current circuit, and ⌘Z brings yours back.

Basics

ExampleWhat to look at
Voltage dividerThe ruler from the original sketch: R2 and R1 split Vdd in a fixed ratio.
RC chargingThe default circuit. At the step all of Vin drops across R1, then moves onto C1.
RLC ringingThe capacitor overshoots Vin while L1's voltage reverses.
Half-wave rectifierD1 conducts only near the peaks, and C1 holds the output up in between.

BJT stages

ExampleWhat to look at
Common-emitter amplifierA small base wiggle swings the collector by about Rc/Re. Built step by step in tutorial 2.
Common emitter, bypassed ReSame bias, but Ce shorts Re for the signal: gain ≈ Rc/re ≈ 250, already distorting at 10 mV.
Common baseThe emitter barely moves, so Rs carries all of Vin and its current goes to the collector.
Emitter followerVBE stays near 0.7 V while the emitter current changes sevenfold.
Current mirrorRL's drop holds still while Vsweep moves, until Q2 saturates at the dip.
Differential amplifierA long-tailed pair. The floating Vo1 → Vo2 output is on a zoomed scale.

MOSFET stages

ExampleWhat to look at
CMOS inverterOne transistor is off while the other is in triode. Mid-ramp, both conduct.
Common sourceGain ≈ gm·RD, with the drain swinging further down than up (square law).
Source followerUnlike the emitter follower, VGS grows visibly with the current.
Common gateThe MOS common base: 1/gm is large, so the source moves with Vin.
CascodeM2 holds M1's drain near 2 V while the output swings by volts.
CMOS differential pairAn NMOS pair with a PMOS mirror load and a single-ended output.

Output and multi-stage

ExampleWhat to look at
Loaded vs buffered stageTwo CE stages drive 1 kΩ, one directly and one through an emitter follower.
Push-pull output stageClass B: the output stalls while |Vin| < 0.6 V (crossover distortion).

Op-amp circuits

ExampleWhat to look at
Inverting op-ampThe − input stays at virtual ground, and the output clips at the rails.
Non-inverting op-ampV− tracks Vin, and Rf and Rg split Vout 2:1.
Summing amplifierThe currents through R1 and R2 add at the virtual ground.
Op-amp integratorA square wave in becomes a triangle out as C1 charges at a constant current.
High-side current senseA tiny floating shunt drop near 12 V, amplified ×20 down to ground.

A good habit with any example: play it once, then select each ruler and read its path in the inspector to see which nodes it measures. Then change one value and watch what moves.
